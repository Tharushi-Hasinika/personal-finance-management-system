# Problem Statement
## Personal Finance Management System

### 1. Background of the Problem

Managing personal finances requires individuals to maintain accurate records of their income, expenses, savings, account balances, and other financial activities. Individuals who use multiple bank accounts, physical cash, and separate savings arrangements need to monitor these financial sources regularly to understand their overall financial situation.

Many individuals rely on manual methods such as spreadsheets, notebooks, or separate applications to manage these activities. Although these methods can be useful for basic record keeping, they often require considerable manual effort and do not provide a centralized view of personal finances.

As financial activities increase, maintaining separate records can make it difficult to monitor transactions, calculate available balances, track savings, and generate meaningful financial summaries.

### 2. Identified Problems

The following problems have been identified with traditional manual personal financial management methods.

#### 2.1 Fragmented Financial Records

Financial information may be maintained across multiple spreadsheets or separate records. This makes it difficult to obtain a complete overview of an individual's financial situation and requires users to refer to different sources when reviewing their finances.

#### 2.2 Manual Transaction Recording and Balance Calculation

Recording transactions and updating account balances manually can be time-consuming. Users must regularly calculate their remaining balances and ensure that each transaction is reflected accurately in the relevant financial records.

#### 2.3 Difficulty in Managing Multiple Financial Sources

Individuals who use multiple bank accounts, physical cash, and separate savings arrangements need to maintain different records for each financial source. Tracking transfers between these sources can become complicated when the records are maintained independently.

#### 2.4 Limited Daily Budget Monitoring

Managing a daily budget requires users to monitor their spending and compare actual expenses against allocated amounts. Without a dedicated mechanism, calculating remaining budgets, tracking unspent amounts, and handling budget overruns can require additional manual calculations.

#### 2.5 Inadequate Savings Tracking

Savings accumulated from different activities may need to be monitored separately. Maintaining these records manually can make it difficult to understand how savings change over time and how they relate to daily spending.

#### 2.6 Difficulty in Tracking Lending Activities

Money lent to other individuals is not an ordinary expense because it represents an amount expected to be repaid. Without a dedicated lending record, users may find it difficult to monitor outstanding amounts, partial repayments, and repayment histories.

#### 2.7 Limited Financial Analysis and Reporting

Manually maintained financial records may not provide convenient summaries of monthly expenses, savings, spending patterns, and outstanding lending amounts. Generating these summaries often requires additional calculations and spreadsheet operations.

#### 2.8 Lack of Synchronized Multi-Device Access

Financial records maintained in local spreadsheets or separate files may not be readily accessible across different devices. Users may need to manually transfer or update files to maintain consistent information.

### 3. Problem Definition

There is a need for a centralized personal finance management solution that simplifies the recording, organization, and monitoring of individual financial activities.

The proposed solution should provide an integrated platform for managing multiple financial accounts, recording income and expenses, monitoring daily budgets, tracking savings, and maintaining lending records.

Furthermore, the system should provide meaningful financial summaries and allow users to access synchronized financial information through web and mobile applications.

The solution should also distinguish between different financial activities, such as expenses, internal transfers, savings movements, lending, and repayments, to ensure that financial records and reports accurately represent an individual's financial position.

### 4. Proposed Solution

The proposed Personal Finance Management System will address these problems by providing a centralized digital platform for personal financial management.

The system will allow users to:

- Maintain financial records for multiple bank accounts and cash wallets.
- Record and categorize income and expenses.
- Monitor account balances and internal transfers.
- Manage daily food budgets and track unspent amounts.
- Maintain separate savings records.
- Record money lent to others and monitor repayments.
- Generate financial summaries and reports.
- Access synchronized financial information through web and mobile applications.

The application will use manual financial data entry rather than direct bank account integration, allowing users to maintain control over the information recorded in the system.
